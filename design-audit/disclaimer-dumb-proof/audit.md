# Disclaimer page — dumb-proof audit

Audited at 1440 × 1000 on 19 August 2026.

## Verdict

Mostly yes. This page is significantly easier to understand than a conventional disclaimer. The warning is prominent and the table consistently explains what each item means and what the user should do.

## Step 1 — Understand the main warning

**Health:** Healthy, with minor repetition.

The dark warning makes the most important boundary unmistakable: an estimate is not proof that tax was filed or paid. The heading, “Your PAYE result is an estimate,” and the warning repeat the same idea three times.

## Step 2 — Understand each limitation

**Health:** Healthy.

The “Your result / What it means / What to do” structure turns legal limitations into practical actions. Calculation, tax advice, exports and rules are separated clearly.

## Step 3 — Choose a next action

**Health:** Mostly healthy.

“See how we calculate” and “Calculate again” are clear. “Privacy” is visually presented as an equal action even though it is a related policy rather than a next step.

## Highest-impact refinements

1. Use one concise introduction: “Your result is an estimate—not proof of filing or payment.”
2. Keep the dark warning, but remove the repeated “Your PAYE result is an estimate” subheading.
3. Keep the two primary next actions beneath the table and make Privacy a smaller related link.
4. On mobile, turn each table row into a labelled card in the order: item, meaning, action.

## Accessibility risks

- The table needs valid table semantics and mobile reflow that preserves the relationship between row and column headings.
- Icon meaning must not be the only way each row is identified; visible text already helps.
- Screenshot evidence cannot confirm focus order, keyboard states, screen-reader table announcements, contrast ratios or zoom behaviour.

## Evidence limits

This is a screenshot-based UX and visual audit. It does not assess the legal completeness or enforceability of the disclaimer.
