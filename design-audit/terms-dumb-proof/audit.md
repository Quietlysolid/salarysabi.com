# Terms page — dumb-proof audit

Audited at 1440 × 1000 on 19 August 2026.

## Verdict

Not yet. The wording is relatively plain for legal content, but the page requires users to read ten sections in sequence before they can identify the responsibilities most relevant to them.

## Step 1 — Understand what the page covers

**Health:** Mostly healthy.

The title and introduction establish that the terms cover calculators, payroll, jobs and accounts. “Terms of use” and “Terms for using SalarySabi” are slightly repetitive.

## Step 2 — Find the important responsibilities

**Health:** Needs improvement.

There is no plain-language summary or contents navigation. The four most important messages are spread across sections 2–5:

- Calculations are estimates, not tax advice.
- Users are responsible for the information they enter and account access.
- SalarySabi reviews job listings but does not guarantee them.
- SalarySabi calculates payroll records but does not make payments, file returns or remit taxes.

## Step 3 — Read the complete terms

**Health:** Mostly healthy.

Numbered headings and borders make individual sections identifiable, and paragraphs are reasonably short. The single long column still feels like a document dump and makes scanning slow.

## Step 4 — Find help or related policies

**Health:** Healthy.

Privacy, tax-changelog and contact links are visibly styled and the contact section tells users not to email sensitive information.

## Highest-impact changes

1. Add a “What you need to know” summary containing the four key responsibilities.
2. Add a compact contents list linking to all ten sections.
3. Shorten the hero to “Terms of use” with one sentence; remove the duplicated eyebrow.
4. Keep the full legal terms below the summary. Do not replace or weaken them.
5. Consider collapsible sections on small screens, provided every term remains directly accessible and printable.

## Accessibility risks

- The long page creates substantial scrolling and navigation effort for keyboard, magnification and cognitive-access users.
- A contents list with skip links would make sections easier to reach.
- Screenshot evidence cannot confirm semantic heading order, focus visibility, link purpose, screen-reader output, zoom reflow or print behaviour.

## Evidence limits

This is a visual and content audit of the rendered desktop page. It is not legal advice and does not assess whether the terms are legally complete or enforceable.
